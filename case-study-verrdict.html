01Decision 01 / 05 · Attention

Tell the reviewer what to open first

A short ranked list of four on landing, ordered by business impact and deadline, with a verb on every row.

What needs attention4 of 18
  1. 1Core switch failure + EOL collisionCORE-SW-NYC-01 · Cisco C9300CriticalView & respond
  2. 2Authorise lifecycle remediation bundle4 devices · LifecycleHighReview
  3. 3Change missing rollback evidenceCHG0038421 · AssuranceHighInspect
  4. 4Evidence expires in 7 days5 records · ComplianceMediumResolve

Ranking rule: business impact first, then time to deadline. Printed under the list, always.

Instead of

Better filters, or sorting the table by severity.

Why

452 open alerts. Filters only help someone who already knows what to look for, and severity alone doesn’t say what to do first.

The cost

Reviewers have to trust a ranking they didn’t set, so the rule is printed under the list.

04Decision 04 / 05 · Time

Warn eighteen months before the deadline

Lifecycle alerts start 18 months out and step up in urgency as the date closes in. Each step names the next action.

Instead of

Alerting on the EOL date, or just showing the date.

Why

Replacing a device takes approvals, a build, testing and a release window. An alert on the date itself is already too late.

The cost

452 open alerts instead of a handful. That’s only workable because the queue is ranked (decision 01).

Reflection

Reflection

What I learned

In regulated work, the interface is the argument.

A screen that can’t explain itself pushes the explanation into email and memory. Designing the reasoning in was the product.

What I’d do next

Test the ranking with real reviewers, and design the escalation path.

I’d put the ranking rules in front of reviewers earlier. The whole queue depends on them trusting it.
